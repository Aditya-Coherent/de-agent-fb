Think of one cake cut into pieces in different ways: by colour, by size, by shape. The pieces always add back to the same cake. Top-down splitting only decides how big each piece is; it never changes the cake. That's why the page has a check that every table adds back to the same total. If one didn't, it would mean a calculation error.

Open ppt/index.html next to this file. The step numbers and names below are the

====================================================================
APPROACH 1: DESK RESEARCH
====================================================================

WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS FOR
Find all the information already published by third parties, competitors or
associations who have calculated the market size: not just the total, but also
the split by segments, applications and the other taxonomy covered in the study.

HOW OURS MATCHES
Same idea. We find the published report for the market, take its market size
and its full taxonomy (segments, applications, regions, countries), and split
the total by all of them.

STEPS (page: 1_Desk_Research_Walkthrough.html, 9 steps)

1. Research & Evidence Gathering
   Find the matching report in the CMI report database and pull live web
   results. An LLM confirms the report is about this market. A regional report
   (for example "GCC Gloves") is rejected when the market is global.

2. Report Taxonomy (Skeleton)
   Take the report's segment list (Product Type, Application, Distribution
   Channel ...) and its regions and countries. These are labels only, no numbers.

3. Market Size + CAGR
   The 2025 (base year) global value and the growth rate (CAGR).
   - If a source states the figure, we use it. A 2026-only figure is taken
     back one year using the CAGR.
   - Code checks the units (billions vs millions).

4. Region & Country Split
   The total is split into regions, then countries.

5. Segment Shares
   Every segment dimension is split (shares add up to 100%).

6. Prices
   A price for each product (used later to turn value into volume).

7. Validation (Review Score)
   Two checks are combined into one score:
   - Consistency (code): do all shares add up to 100%, and do the regions add up?
   - Plausibility (LLM judge): do the numbers make sense?
   If the score is below 90%, go back to step 3 and redo steps 3 to 6 with
   the reviewer's comments. At least 2 and at most 4 passes. The best pass is
   kept.

8. Justification (Brain)
   A written explanation of why the numbers make sense.

9. Final Market Size -> Input Sheet -> ME Workbook
   The result is written into the Input Sheet. The ME pipeline then expands it
   to Value, ASP and Volume for 2021 to 2033.

OLIVE OIL RESULT
- Scores by pass: 82%, 84%, 82%, 83%.
- None reached 90%, so the loop stopped after 4 passes and kept pass 2.
- Final: $14.6B (2025), split across 5 regions, 23 countries and 3 segment
  types.
- Note: this run found no published figure, so the value is an estimate.


====================================================================
APPROACH 2A: DEMAND SIDE - COUNTRY TOTAL, THEN SEGMENTS TOP-DOWN
====================================================================

WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS FOR
Track the demand of the market at country level, add it up (bottom up) to
global, then do a top-down analysis for all the sub-segments.

HOW OURS MATCHES
Exactly that. One demand number per country (quantity x price), countries
added up to global, then every segment split top-down from the country totals.

STEPS (page: 2A_Demand_Country_Total_Walkthrough.html, 15 steps)

1. Market Setup
   Market, list of countries, base year, unit (for example tonnes).

2. Scope Statement
   What is inside and outside this market.

3. Geography
   The approved list of countries, each mapped to its region.

4. Total-Demand Driver
   What we measure in each country. For olive oil this is consumption in
   tonnes (production + imports - exports).

5. Evidence Search & Retrieval
   For each country, try sources in order: statistics offices, FAO, trade data,
   industry bodies. Each number gets a label:
   - verified (found in a source)
   - derived (calculated from verified numbers)
   - A (no source found, an estimate)

6. Quantity per Country
   The demand number for every country, each with its label.

7. Pricing
   The price per unit (olive oil: $4,200 per tonne).

8. Calculation (Quantity x Price)
   Country value = quantity x price. Done by code, not by the LLM.

9. Country -> Region -> Global
   Countries are added into regions, and regions into the global total.

10. Cross-Check vs Published Estimates
   Our total is compared with market sizes published online. This only raises
   a warning; it never changes our number.

11. Segment Taxonomy
   The segments to split: Product Type, Application, Distribution Channel,
   Form.

12. Segment Shares per Country
   The share of each segment in each country, from web-backed estimates. In
   every country the shares must add up to 100%.

13. Top-Down Split (Country Total x Share)
   Segment value = country total x segment share. Done for every country and
   every segment.

14. Final Market Size
   Global, regions, countries, plus one table per segment. Every table adds
   back to the same global total.

15. Review as Market Researcher
   Automatic warnings that point to anything suspicious.

OLIVE OIL RESULT
- Built on official data, no estimates:
  - quantities: International Olive Council balance 2024/25 (world 3,215,000
    tonnes, country by country);
  - price: Spain extra virgin, Sept 2025, EUR 4.315/kg x 1.1308 = $4,879/t.
- Southern Europe (Spain, Italy, Portugal, Greece) $4.8B, rest of EU $2.4B,
  US $2.1B, Turkiye $1.0B, Morocco $0.7B and others.
- Global total 2025: $15.7B.
- Published 2025 estimates: IMARC $15.7B, Straits $18.75B, SNS $19.8B.


====================================================================
APPROACH 2B: DEMAND SIDE - APPLICATION x COUNTRY, OTHER SEGMENTS TOP-DOWN
====================================================================
Why this happens: country totals are published (IOC), but use-case figures per country, like "olive oil used by restaurants in Italy", mostly aren't. It's the same data problem as 2C, one level higher.


WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS FOR
Track demand at country level, across each end-use application in each
country. Add the applications up to the country, and the countries up to
global. Other segments are done top-down.

HOW OURS MATCHES
Exactly that. Demand is measured for each application in each country,
applications are added up to the country, and countries to global. The
application table stays bottom-up; the other segments are split top-down.

STEPS (page: 2B_Demand_UseCase_Country_Walkthrough.html, 16 steps)

1 to 3. Market Setup, Scope Statement, Geography
   Same as 2A.

4. Use-Case Taxonomy
   The end-use applications. For olive oil: Home Cooking, Foodservice,
   Processed Food, Infused Oils, Nutraceutical/Pharma. A scope check removes
   any application that does not really use this product.

5. Demand Driver per Use Case
   What we measure for each application. For example, foodservice = meals per
   year x oil per meal. Two applications are never allowed to share one driver.

6. Evidence Search & Retrieval
   Same as 2A, done for every application in every country.

7. Quantity per Use Case x Country
   The demand number for every application in every country.

8. Pricing
   The price per unit for each application. Code catches a box price applied
   to single items, and price outliers.

9. Calculation (Quantity x Price)
   Value per application per country. Done by code. Unit conversions (for
   example litres to tonnes) are also done by code.

10. Use Cases -> Country -> Region -> Global
   Applications are added into the country, countries into regions, regions
   into global.

11. Cross-Check vs Published Estimates
   Same as 2A.

12 to 14. Segment Taxonomy, Segment Shares per Country, Top-Down Split
   The other segments (Grade, Extraction Method, Channel, Packaging) are split
   top-down, the same way as in 2A. The application table stays bottom-up.

15. Final Market Size
   Every table adds back to the same global total.

16. Review as Market Researcher
   Automatic warnings.

OLIVE OIL RESULT
- 5 applications x 14 countries, global total $9.3B.
- WARNING: foodservice is 64% ($5.9B) and home cooking only $0.34B. That
  looks wrong, and the system flagged it.
- All inputs in this run are estimates. Treat this as the weakest olive oil
  number.

APPROACH 2C: DEMAND SIDE - PRODUCT TYPE x APPLICATION x COUNTRY
====================================================================

WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS FOR
Like B, but inside each application also track demand for each product type.
Product types add up to the application, applications to the country,
countries to global. The rest of the segments are done top-down.

HOW OURS MATCHES
Shown as two walkthrough pages, not run as a pipeline.

PAGE 1: 2C_Demand_Product_UseCase_Country_Walkthrough.html (olive oil, 9 steps)
1. Market Setup
2. Use Cases (Applications)
3. Product Types inside Each Use Case (Extra Virgin, Virgin, Refined, Pomace)
4. Product Volume Mix per Country
5. Price per Product Type
6. Calculation (Product Quantity x Product Price)
7. Products -> Use Case (per Country)
8. Use Cases -> Country -> Region -> Global
9. Final Market Size
The quantities come from the real 2B run. The price differences between
grades are illustrative.

PAGE 2: 2C_Vinegar_India_White_Vinegar_Walkthrough.html (11 steps, inputs
checked online)
1. Market Setup: white vinegar, India
2. Trade Check: imports and exports are tiny, so India makes its own vinegar
3. Top-Down Anchor: the total white vinegar used in India (about 291,000
   tonnes)
4. White Vinegar Use Cases: which uses have published data and which don't
5. Use Case: Ketchup = 1.2 million t of ketchup x 8% vinegar x 70%
6. Use Case: Mayonnaise = mayonnaise volume x 5.4% vinegar
7. Use Case: Cakes = 378,000 t of cakes x 39.5% eggless x 2% vinegar
   = 2,987 t of vinegar
8. Price -> Revenue: tonnes x Rs 42.25 per litre
9. Reconciliation: these 3 uses explain 28% of India's white vinegar; the
   rest has no published data
10. Result: white vinegar in cakes in India is about Rs 12.5 crore
    (US$1.5M)
11. Source Audit: every input listed with its website and whether it was
    verified

WHY IT IS HARD
- Vinegar doses in recipes and adoption rates are rarely published.
- The number of cells explodes: product types x applications x countries.
- Each cell is small, so researching it can cost more than it is worth.


====================================================================
APPROACH 3: SUPPLY SIDE
====================================================================

WHAT THE BRIEF ASKS FOR
Analyse the companies that earn revenue directly in this industry (not across
the whole value chain):
1. analyse them
2. get their total revenue
3. keep only the part of their revenue that comes from this market (Google:
   only its cloud API revenue, not all of Google)
Then add them up. This also gives company market shares.

HOW OURS MATCHES
Exactly those three steps, then the sum, then the company market shares.

STEPS (page: 3_Supply_Side_Walkthrough.html, 9 steps)

1. Vendor Discovery
   Find the companies in this market (LLM + web). Check each website, to catch
   made-up names.

2. Screening: Direct Revenue Contributors Only
   - Remove distributors and retailers: they resell other companies'
     products, so they would be counted twice.
   - Merge brands into their parent company.

3. Company Total Revenue
   Real revenue from financial data (Yahoo Finance, InsightSentry), converted
   to US dollars.

4. Revenue Attributable to This Market, per Country
   Only the part of each company's revenue that comes from this market, split
   by country. It is capped by the company's real revenue.

5. Gross-Up for Vendors Not Named
   In each country, estimate what share of the market the named companies
   cover, then divide by that share. This adds the companies we did not name.
   The share is never allowed below 30%.

6. Company Market Shares
   Each company's revenue divided by the market total.

7. Cross-Check: Published Market Sizes
   The middle value of market sizes quoted online (no LLM involved).

8. Final Market Size
   All countries added up.

9. Review as Market Researcher
   Automatic warnings.

OLIVE OIL RESULT
- 33 companies after merging brands. For example, Bertolli, Carbonell,
  Carapelli and Sasso are counted once, under Deoleo.
- 17 countries, total $8.2B. Published figures say $14.0B.
- Top shares: Aceites del Sur 14%, Sovena 12%, Deoleo 10%.
- Only 2 of 33 companies have public financial data; most are private.

====================================================================
THINGS TO SAY UPFRONT
====================================================================

1. When no source exists, the LLM makes an estimate. Every number carries a
   label saying whether it is verified, derived or A (no source found).
2. Results can change between runs, when web search finds different sources.
3. All the arithmetic (quantity x price, sums, splits) is done by code, never
   by the LLM.
4. The warnings only flag a number for a person to check. They never change
   the number on their own.
