The core reframe
Revenue = Volume × Price. You never need to ask anyone for "$10.0M." You need Volume and Price, and the revenue falls out as arithmetic. So the question becomes: can we derive 2.5M litres and $4.00/L from hard data? Mostly yes.

Price per litre — this is the easy one, genuinely derivable
UN Comtrade publishes trade under HS code 2209 — "Vinegar and substitutes for vinegar obtained from acetic acid" — free, per country, per year, with both value (USD) and quantity (kg/litres). Divide them and you get a real unit value $/litre, by trade partner. Balsamic ≈ India's 2209 imports from Italy → a defensible landed price with a source URL.
Retail price scraping — Amazon India, BigBasket, Blinkit listings for balsamic: extract price + pack size → price-per-litre distribution (median, weighted by review count as a crude volume proxy).
Landed price × distribution markup ↔ retail price gives you a two-sided sanity check.
No LLM anywhere in that chain.

Volume — derivable via the classic identity
Apparent consumption = Domestic Production + Imports − Exports.

This is the standard, auditable market-sizing identity real analysts use. Imports/exports come straight from Comtrade. Production comes from national statistics offices / industry associations (this is the genuine gap for some countries — more below).

Then per-capita consumption = apparent consumption ÷ population, which you sanity-check against neighbours (India's litres/capita should sit in a plausible band vs. other South Asian markets, not 10× off).

Where it gets progressively harder — be honest about this
Layer	Derivable?	Source
Price $/L	Easy	Comtrade unit values + retail scraping
Country total volume	Medium	Comtrade + production stats (production is the gap)
Product mix (balsamic's share)	Medium-hard	Trade sub-codes, import origin (Italy→balsamic), retail assortment scraping
Application mix (F&B vs Cleaning)	Hardest	No direct data exists — needs input-output tables, industry ratios, or a modeled allocation
That last row is the honest catch: nobody publishes "how much vinegar went into cleaning vs. food in India." You cannot derive it from trade data. But you can replace an LLM guess with a documented, parameterized assumption (e.g. food-grade vs. industrial-grade import classification, packaging-size distribution as a proxy — 5L jugs skew industrial/cleaning, 250ml bottles skew retail food). That's defensible in a way "the LLM said 68%" never is.

Where your ML model actually earns its place
Comtrade covers most countries but not all, and production data is patchy. So:

Build the hard-data pipeline for the countries where Comtrade + production do exist (that's your training set with real labels — not LLM pseudo-labels).
Fit the regression on those real observations (population, GDP/capita, urbanization, food-industry output → volume).
Predict only for the gap countries. That's legitimate statistical interpolation across a real dataset — completely different from distilling an LLM's guesses, and it's the defensible answer to "where did this number come from" for the countries with no data.
The big lever: this machinery already exists in your repo
The production demand_usage pipeline already has exactly this architecture — evidence_router.py with web_acquirer / pdf_acquirer / tabular_acquirer / api_acquirer, and a provenance system tagging every number as verified_observed / derived / source_found_unverified / assumption. The Segmentation Demo deliberately skipped all of it for speed. Pointing an api_acquirer at Comtrade and a web_acquirer at retail listings is reusing something you already built and tested, not starting over.

The blunt framing for the CEO
You can eliminate the LLM from magnitudes. You cannot eliminate assumptions — application mix in particular has no published source anywhere. What you can deliver is: every number carries a provenance tag and a source URL, assumptions are explicit and parameterized (so he can challenge or change them), and the audit trail shows exactly which numbers are hard-sourced vs. modeled vs. assumed. That's a much stronger position than "trust the AI," and it's achievable.
