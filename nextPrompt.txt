1. BACKGROUND (what was asked and found)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Question: Desk Research (approach 1, src/me_engine/generation/) showed ~10% median error
vs actuals_2025.csv even though all numbers come from LLM/web search. Why?

Findings:
- The "actual" in actuals_2025.csv is the median of 2-3 published reports, which themselves
  disagree by ~11% typical (e.g. watches 78.8-124.5 Bn, urea 49.7-79.7 Bn). So ~10% is near the
  noise floor; any single published report would score about the same.
- Originally only ~3 of 7 Desk values were grounded in a cited source; the rest were LLM guesses.
- Televisions (-88%): the DB lookup (db_source.py pick_best_report) matched "Curved Televisions
  Market" (CMI DB newsId 4674, USD 10.32 Bn in 2026). Its segmentation in the DB is corrupt
  ("Cable Track Cables / Torsion Cables"). The first pass guessed 7.8 Bn (ungrounded); later
  passes guessed 245-258 Bn; the reviewer (plausibility score) kept 7.8 Bn (64% vs 60%).
- Urea (-58%): grounded on CMI "Urea Fertilizers Market" (newsId 9620, USD 32.71 Bn in 2026 ->
  31,242 for 2025). The actual (73,500) is TOTAL urea (fertilizer + industrial) -> scope mismatch.
  The CMI 32.7 Bn figure itself also looks low vs volume x price.
- Side note: semantic_search() in semantic_search.py errors on the DB
  ("operator does not exist: vector <=> double precision[]"); nearest_report() is written
  differently. It was not checked whether anything calls semantic_search().
