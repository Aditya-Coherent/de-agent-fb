====================================================================================
TURN 1 — QUESTION
====================================================================================
User: "In the desk research approach when I'm comparing with the actual data then median
error is coming around 10% which is huge while all the numbers is coming from the llm or
web search then why so much error?"

ANSWER (summary)
- The ~10% is close to the floor. Each "actual" in actuals_2025.csv is the median of 2-3
  published reports, and those reports disagree with each other. A single real published
  report sits a median of ~11% from that median. Examples:
    Smartphones 537,620 / 585,630 / 598,920 (+/-8%)
    Watches     78,800 / 81,300 / 124,500 (up to 53%)
    Olive Oil   13,590 / 15,700 / 18,750 (up to 19%)
    Gloves       8,500 (CMI) / 13,800 / 15,860 (up to 38%)
    Urea        49,720 / 73,500 / 79,730 (up to 32%)
- Only ~3 of 7 Desk values actually came from a cited figure; the rest were LLM guesses.
  Desk errors: Smartphones -4.9% (cited), Watches -15% (cited, different publisher),
  Smartwatches +10.5% (only CAGR cited), Olive Oil -6.9% (ungrounded), Gloves +10.5%
  (ungrounded), Televisions -87.9% (ungrounded; passes gave 7.8 / 258 / 245 / 245 Bn and the
  reviewer kept 7.8 Bn because it scored 64% vs 60%), Urea -57.5% (scope mismatch:
  fertilizer-only vs total urea).
- Suggested fixes: pick passes by groundedness, flag unstable values, extract cited figures
  in code and take a median of several, check scope, measure "within published range".

OPEN ITEMS (found on held-out failures, deliberately NOT changed)
====================================================================================
1. Televisions +333%: research sites report $119-415 Bn; the "actual" is two hardware-only
   sources at ~$64 Bn. A definition disagreement, not a parsing error.
2. Olive Oil +39%: IMARC is not recognised as a research firm by the general tier rule (no
   keyword in the domain, no report path in the URL), so tier-2 copies joined the median.
3. Surgical Masks +33%: a generic "masks" figure was accepted; the keyword rule lets a
   two-word market drop one word.
4. Markets with no DB report are less accurate: ~10% median error on held-out, ~31% on
   tuning (e.g. Wireless Earbuds +121%, Digital Cameras -57%).
5. The LLM scope judge wrongly rejected Fortune's total-urea figure as "narrower".
6. Web search (ddgs) returns different results each run, so part of every difference is
   search noise.
Any fix for 1-3 should be judged on a fresh held-out set, since these were found by looking
at held-out results.
